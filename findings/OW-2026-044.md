# OW-2026-044 · Recovery could rebroadcast a different signed Bitcoin-family payment

> Current audit target: [5.0.2 (104) prerelease](../review/RELEASE-5.0.2-104.md). Earlier results below retain their original tested versions and commits; they are not retroactively tests of build 104.

Low severity. Reproduction required two already-signed transactions and the ability to substitute one for the other in the local payment journal. A peer returning empty history was sufficient to reach the retry decision once the journal had been altered. The test did not demonstrate a remote write, a signature forgery or a transfer of funds.

The [affected recovery reader](https://github.com/devdasx/oath-wallet/blob/8c4e1d5d3a14bec0b9ea3a1fd01c7611ea4dd707/Packages/OathBitcoin/Sources/OathBitcoin/BitcoinSendStatus.swift#L31-L46) compared the inputs against the payment plan. The [retry path](https://github.com/devdasx/oath-wallet/blob/8c4e1d5d3a14bec0b9ea3a1fd01c7611ea4dd707/OathAtmosphere/Send/OathBitcoinSendService.swift#L137-L153) calculated the identity from the same stored bytes that it might submit. A second transaction spending the same inputs to a different recipient therefore passed the local check.

The test signed two synthetic payments, substituted the second in SQLite, and supplied an empty history to the production recovery reader. Before the fix the substituted transaction was eligible for rebroadcast. The expected behavior is to reject it before consulting a peer or advancing the journal. [Original assertion](../evidence/progress56/before/testRecoveryRejectsSignedBytesForAnotherPaymentUsingTheSameInputs.log) · [Same fixture after repair](../evidence/progress56/after/testRecoveryRejectsSignedBytesForAnotherPaymentUsingTheSameInputs.log).

The [repair](https://github.com/devdasx/oath-wallet/commit/c78b18fa42319d0a30963a10da1e9336f722fb67) checks one database snapshot against the reviewed recipient, amount, network, transaction plan and independently stored transaction ID. It stores a witness-inclusive digest with new signatures. A changed witness can preserve a Bitcoin transaction ID. For older entries without the new digest, only the original transaction ID and the plan are available. The checks do not authenticate a database against an actor able to rewrite every commitment.
